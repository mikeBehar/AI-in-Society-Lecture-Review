Lecture Summary

This introductory lecture establishes the foundational frameworks for understanding ethical considerations in artificial intelligence. As AI systems become increasingly integrated into critical decision-making processes across healthcare, criminal justice, finance, and education, the need for robust ethical guidelines has never been more urgent.

The lecture explores three major ethical frameworks that can be applied to AI development: consequentialism (evaluating AI by its outcomes), deontology (focusing on duties and rules), and virtue ethics (emphasizing the character and intentions of AI developers). Each framework offers unique insights but also presents challenges when applied to complex AI systems.

Key Points

  • The Autonomy Challenge: As AI systems become more autonomous, questions arise about accountability and responsibility when things go wrong. Who is responsible when an autonomous vehicle causes an accident?
  • The Transparency Problem: Many modern AI systems, particularly deep learning models, operate as "black boxes" where even their creators cannot fully explain their decision-making processes. This poses significant ethical challenges for high-stakes applications.
  • Value Alignment: How do we ensure AI systems reflect human values when humans themselves disagree on fundamental ethical principles? The lecture examines the challenge of encoding diverse human values into algorithmic systems.
  • The Trolley Problem 2.0: Classic ethical dilemmas take on new dimensions in AI contexts. The lecture uses autonomous vehicles as a case study for examining how AI must be programmed to handle moral dilemmas in split-second decisions.
  • Global Perspectives: Ethical considerations vary across cultures. The lecture emphasizes the importance of incorporating diverse global perspectives in AI ethics rather than imposing Western-centric values universally.
  • Stakeholder Analysis: Identifying all stakeholders affected by AI systems—from end users to communities to future generations—and considering their interests in the design process.

Discussion Topics

  • Can we create universal ethical principles for AI, or must ethics remain contextual and culturally specific?
  • How do we balance innovation and safety in AI development? When is it appropriate to deploy AI systems, and when should we wait?
  • What role should the public play in determining the ethical guidelines for AI systems that affect their lives?
  • How can we ensure that ethical considerations are not just theoretical but are practically implemented in AI development workflows?
